# Guest Right

Guest right is the oldest and most sacred law of hospitality in Westeros. It binds host and guest alike, and the gods are held to punish its breach.

## The Custom

A guest who eats the host's bread and salt, or drinks under his roof, is under the protection of guest right. The host may not harm the guest, and the guest may not harm the host or his household, for as long as the guest is under the roof. The right also protects a guest for three days after he leaves the roof.

## Beginning and End of Guest Right

### How It Begins

Guest right begins when the guest has taken bread and salt from the host's own hand or table. A guest who has been offered but has not eaten is not yet under guest right, although it is shameful to harm him.

### How It Ends

Guest right ends three days after the guest leaves the roof, or when the guest himself breaks the right by attacking the host. A host may order a guest to leave but may not drive him out by force while he is under the roof.

## Violations and Sanctions

A person who kills or harms a guest, or a host who harms a guest, breaks guest right. The offender is accursed, loses the protection of the law, and is not entitled to [trial by combat](trial_by_combat.md#when-it-may-be-demanded). Any lord may hang him without trial. The Crown does not [override guest right](crown_law.md#sacred-customs-the-crown-will-not-override). The offense is also a form of [murder](crimes_and_punishments.md#murder) and may be charged as such.

### Breaches by Lords

A lord who breaks guest right against a noble guest may also be charged with [treason](treason_and_attainder.md#what-is-treason) if the guest was a servant of the Crown or was under the king's peace.

## Regional Variations

In the North guest right is held to be the strongest of all laws, and a breach is punished by the lord of the land even if the Crown takes no action. In the Riverlands guest right is observed but is commonly applied only to guests who have shared a meal. In the Iron Islands a salt oath is required before guest right begins.
