# Courts, Evidence and Procedure

This article describes how a case moves from accusation to sentence and what may be done afterward.

## Accusation and Arrest

Any freeborn person may accuse another before the lord of the land, but an accusation must name the accuser, the accused, the offense, the place, and the day. An anonymous accusation is void. The lord's bailiff may hold the accused for up to seven days before a hearing is held. An accused lord, knight or member of a sworn order may not be held in the common cells but is kept under guard in a chamber of the keep.

## Evidence and Witnesses

### Oaths

Witnesses swear before a septon, or in the North before a heart tree. A witness who swears falsely commits [perjury](crimes_and_punishments.md#perjury-and-false-witness).

### The Two-Witness Rule

No person may be sentenced to death on the word of a single witness. A capital conviction requires two sworn witnesses to the act, a confession made freely before a septon, or the outcome of a [trial by combat](trial_by_combat.md) or a [trial by seven](trial_by_seven.md).

### Written Testimony

A witness who is old, ill or far away may give sworn testimony in writing, sealed by a maester. Written testimony counts as one witness for the two-witness rule only if the accused had notice of it.

## The Judge and the Panel

In a capital case the lord sits as judge with a maester to record the proceedings and a septon to administer the oaths. In a case of low justice a steward or bailiff may sit alone. The accused has the right to speak last. See [the lord as judge](lordly_justice.md#the-lord-as-judge).

## Verdicts

The judge states the verdict and the sentence in the same hearing. A sentence of death must be carried out no sooner than three days and no later than one moon after the verdict, to allow time for an appeal.

## Appeals and Royal Review

An appeal from a lord's judgment goes first to the lord paramount, and from there to the king or the Hand. An appeal must be made within a fortnight of the verdict. An appeal of a capital sentence suspends execution for up to one moon. Appeals in the Crownlands go to the Justices of the Crown; see [the Master of Laws](crown_law.md#the-master-of-laws). The king alone may grant [a pardon](royal_mercy.md#the-royal-pardon).

## Judgment by Ordeal

When the evidence is deadlocked, an accused person in a capital case may demand a trial by combat. A knight whose honor is at stake may instead ask for a trial by seven, if the Crown allows. See [when combat may be demanded](trial_by_combat.md#when-it-may-be-demanded) and [the form of the trial by seven](trial_by_seven.md#the-form-of-the-trial).
