# Royal Mercy, Pardon and Commutation

Mercy, like justice, flows from the Iron Throne. The king may spare any person condemned in his realm, subject to the limits set out below.

## The Royal Pardon

### Who May Grant a Pardon

Only the king may grant a pardon. The Hand, the Master of Laws, and the lords paramount may recommend one but may not grant it; see [the Hand's delegation](crown_law.md#the-hands-delegation).

### Form of a Pardon

A pardon must be written, sealed with the royal seal, and proclaimed by a herald in the place where the sentence was passed. A pardon granted in private, without a herald, is void and has no effect on the sentence.

## Commutation

The king may commute a sentence to a lesser one. The most common commutation of a death sentence is service at the Wall; see [pardon by service](night_watch_and_the_wall.md#pardon-by-service). The king may also commute a sentence of death to exile for a term of years, or to the payment of a heavy fine.

## Limits of the Pardon

A royal pardon:

- does not cancel a blood price owed to the kin of a victim; see [fines and blood price](crimes_and_punishments.md#fines-and-blood-price);
- does not restore the lands or titles of an attainted house, which requires a separate decree of restoration under [attainder and forfeiture](treason_and_attainder.md#attainder-and-forfeiture);
- cannot release a sworn brother of the Night's Watch from his vow, or a deserter from the penalty, without the Lord Commander's consent;
- does not bind the king's successor, who may revoke a pardon granted by a prior king if it has not yet been proclaimed.

## Heralds and Proclamations

A herald who proclaims a pardon must read the full text and name the person pardoned. The herald is protected by the king's peace and may not be harmed; see [violations by lords](war_and_siege_law.md#violations-by-lords). A pardon proclaimed in the Crownlands takes effect at once; elsewhere it takes effect when it reaches the judge who passed the sentence.
