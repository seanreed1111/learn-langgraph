# Marriage and Family Law

Marriage joins two houses and is regulated both by the law of the Crown and by the rites of the Faith.

## Betrothal

A betrothal is a written contract between two houses, signed by both lords and sealed. A betrothal made without the consent of the lord who is the bride's or groom's guardian is void. A betrothal may be broken by mutual consent or by the lord paramount's order on proof that one party has broken the terms.

## Septon Rites and Northern Rites

A marriage is valid if it is witnessed by at least two persons and consecrated by a septon, who binds the couple's hands. In the North a couple may instead wed before a heart tree, with the lord or his steward as witness. Both forms are recognized throughout the realm, and a marriage valid in one form is valid everywhere. See [church courts](faith_and_septs.md#church-courts).

## Dowry and Dower

The bride's house pays a dowry to the groom's house. The groom's house in turn sets aside lands for the bride's support if she is widowed. This is the dower, and it is described in [property and dower](succession_and_inheritance.md#property-and-dower). A widow who remarries keeps her dower for life but may not pass it to the heirs of the new husband.

## Annulment and Dissolution

### When Annulment Is Allowed

Marriage cannot ordinarily be dissolved. Annulment is allowed only for nonconsummation, for lack of free consent, for a prior living spouse, or for kinship within three degrees. Only the High Septon may grant an annulment, and he does so usually at the king's request.

### Dornish Dissolution

In Dorne a marriage may be dissolved by mutual consent before a magistrate of the Prince. See [Dorne](regional_customs.md#dorne).

## Kinslaying and Family Duties

A person who kills a kinsman commits the offense of [kinslaying](crimes_and_punishments.md#kinslaying). A father must provide for his trueborn children, and a lord who neglects his heir may be summoned before his lord paramount.

## Wardship and Fostering

### Fostering

Noble children are often sent to be fostered in another house. The foster-parent is bound to protect the child and to treat him as one of his own. A foster-parent who harms a ward breaks his trust and may be charged as a murderer if the ward dies.

### Foster-Kin

In the North a foster-sibling counts as kin for the purposes of the law of kinslaying. Elsewhere a foster-sibling is treated as a stranger. See [kinslaying](crimes_and_punishments.md#kinslaying) and [the North](regional_customs.md#the-north).

### Wards of the Crown

A lord's heir who is orphaned becomes the ward of his lord paramount. See [regency and wardship](succession_and_inheritance.md#regency-and-wardship).
