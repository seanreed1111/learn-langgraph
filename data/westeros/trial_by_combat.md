# Trial by Combat

Trial by combat is an ancient ordeal in which the gods are believed to give victory to the innocent. It is the oldest form of judgment still in use in the Seven Kingdoms.

## Origin and Purpose

The First Men judged disputes by single combat before the heart trees, and the Andals brought the custom to the south, where the Faith gave it a new meaning. Trial by combat settles a case in which the evidence is deadlocked and the accused swears he is innocent. It is meant as a last resort and not as a first choice.

## When It May Be Demanded

### Capital Cases

Any person accused of a capital crime may demand trial by combat, and the judge may not refuse. This right applies to [murder](crimes_and_punishments.md#murder), to [treason](treason_and_attainder.md#combat-and-confession), and to every other crime punishable by death. In lesser cases combat may be held only if both parties agree.

### Who May Demand It

Only the accused may demand combat. The accuser cannot force the accused to fight, but the accuser may name a champion to fight on his behalf if he is the one who stands to lose.

### When It Is Denied

The right is lost by a person who has confessed. It may not be demanded in a case that belongs to a church court, such as heresy; see [church courts](faith_and_septs.md#church-courts). A judge may refuse a demand made after the verdict has been spoken.

## Champions and Substitutes

An accused person may fight himself or name a champion. A woman, a child, a cripple, or an old man may always name a champion. A champion who is paid does not forfeit his honor, but a knight of the Kingsguard may not serve as a champion except for the king; see [duties in court](kingsguard_and_oaths.md#duties-in-court).

## Conduct of the Combat

The combat is held in the open before the judge and is witnessed by a septon, who asks the Seven to give victory to the innocent. The combatants fight with arms of their own choosing and with armor, unless both agree otherwise. In a capital case the combat is to the death. In lesser cases it ends when one combatant yields.

## Outcome and Consequences

The victor is declared innocent, and the charge is dismissed. If the accused wins, the accuser's champion is dead or yielded and the accuser is not punished unless the accusation was malicious; see [perjury](crimes_and_punishments.md#perjury-and-false-witness). If the accused loses, the sentence is carried out at once and no appeal is permitted. The king may still extend [royal mercy](royal_mercy.md#the-royal-pardon) after the combat is lost.
