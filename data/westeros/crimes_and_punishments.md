# Crimes and Punishments

This article lists the common offenses and the penalties the law attaches to them. Lords with only low justice may impose only the lesser penalties; see [the powers of low justice](lordly_justice.md#the-powers-of-low-justice).

## Homicide

### Murder

The deliberate killing of another person is murder and is punished by death. A noble is beheaded and a commoner is hanged. A person accused of murder may demand [trial by combat](trial_by_combat.md#when-it-may-be-demanded).

### Kinslaying

Killing a member of one's own family is kinslaying, the gravest of all crimes in the eyes of gods and men. The penalty is death, and the right to ask for the Wall is withheld unless the Lord Commander of the Night's Watch agrees; see [who is accepted](night_watch_and_the_wall.md#who-is-accepted). In the North a foster-brother or fostered ward counts as kin for this purpose; elsewhere such a killing is charged as murder. See [fostering](marriage_and_family_law.md#wardship-and-fostering).

### Killing in Defense

A person who kills to defend his life, his household, or his lord's guest is not guilty of murder, but he must still pay the blood price to the dead man's kin. The amount is set by private compact between the houses; see [blood price](#fines-and-blood-price) below.

## Theft and Robbery

Theft of goods worth less than a silver stag is punished with flogging. Theft of greater value is punished by the loss of the right hand. A third theft, or any theft with violence, is punished by death or, at the thief's choice, the Wall. A lord who holds only low justice may not take a hand; he must send the thief to a lord with the right of pit and gallows.

## Poaching and Forest Law

Hunting the lord's game without leave is a fine and a day in the stocks on the first offense. On a second offense the poacher loses the right hand. Hunting the king's game is treated as theft from the Crown.

## Arson

Burning a dwelling while someone is inside is murder. Burning an empty building or a field is punished by flogging and by repayment of twice the value of the loss.

## Perjury and False Witness

A witness who swears falsely in a trial commits perjury. If the false oath helped bring about a death sentence, the perjurer suffers the same punishment as the condemned man. Otherwise the perjurer is flogged and loses the right to testify ever again.

## Offenses Against Women

The rape of a woman is punished by death or by castration and, at the victim's choice, by the payment of a dowry to her house. A man convicted of rape may not take the black.

## Punishments

### Death and Method

A noble who is condemned to death is beheaded by the sword. A commoner is hanged. A traitor may be drawn and quartered only by a decree of the king; see [attainder](treason_and_attainder.md#attainder-and-forfeiture). The lord passing sentence must follow the timetable in [verdicts](courts_and_procedure.md#verdicts).

### The Black

A person condemned for a lesser capital crime such as theft with violence, poaching, or assault may take the black and serve for life at the Wall instead of dying. Kinslayers, rapists, and unpardoned traitors are not accepted. See [taking the black](night_watch_and_the_wall.md#taking-the-black).

### Maiming

Maiming, most often the loss of a hand, is a punishment for theft and poaching. Only a lord with high justice may order it.

### Fines and Blood Price

A fine is paid to the lord. Blood price, sometimes called wergild, is paid to the family of the dead or injured person. The amounts vary with the rank of the victim and are fixed by private compact among the lords. A pardon from the king does not cancel a blood price owed to a victim's kin; see [limits of the pardon](royal_mercy.md#limits-of-the-pardon).

### Exile and Outlawry

A person may be exiled from a lord's lands for a term of years or for life. An outlaw is a person who has fled judgment. The lord may name him outlaw by proclamation, after which any person may kill him without penalty.
