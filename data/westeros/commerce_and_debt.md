# Commerce, Contracts and Debt

Trade moves goods and coin between the Seven Kingdoms and across the Narrow Sea. This article states the rules that protect the honest merchant and the honest debtor.

## Contracts and Seals

A contract is binding if it is written, sealed by both parties, and witnessed by two persons. An oral contract is valid only if the value is less than ten silver stags and is witnessed by two persons. A contract signed under threat is void. A forged seal is an offense; if it is the royal seal, it is [treason](treason_and_attainder.md#what-is-treason).

## Debts and Creditors

### Summons and Seizure

A creditor may summon a debtor before the lord of the debtor's land. After three unanswered summons the lord may seize goods or land of the debtor up to the value of the debt. A creditor may not seize a debtor's weapons, tools of trade, or a lord's seat.

### Imprisonment for Debt

A debtor who cannot pay may be held for up to a year and a day, after which the debt may be satisfied by service. A lord with only low justice may impose this penalty; see [the powers of low justice](lordly_justice.md#the-powers-of-low-justice).

## The Iron Bank

The Iron Bank of Braavos lends to lords and to the Crown. A debt owed by a king is binding on his successors, and the Crown may not lawfully disown a loan sealed with the royal seal. The Iron Bank is subject to the law of the land in which it holds property, but it may not be tried by a lord for the failure of a loan.

## Tolls and Customs

Roads, bridges, and ports may be tolled by the lord of the land. A toll may not exceed what is customary and must be posted at the crossing. A lord may not toll a king's herald or a royal messenger.

## Guild Law

Guilds of merchants and craftsmen set their own standards and settle disputes among their members. A guild court may fine a member but may not imprison him. Matters of felony remain with the lord's justice; see [lordly justice](lordly_justice.md).

## Coin and Weights

Gold dragons, silver stags, and copper pennies are the coin of the realm. A person who clips or counterfeits the king's coin commits treason; see [what is treason](treason_and_attainder.md#what-is-treason). Weights and measures are checked by the lord's steward, and a merchant who uses false weights is fined and loses his right to trade for a year.
