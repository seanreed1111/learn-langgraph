# Treason and Attainder

Treason is an offense against the Crown itself. Only the king, or the Hand with the king's written consent, may try it.

## What Is Treason

The following acts are treason:

- levying war against the king or taking up arms against his banners;
- giving aid, shelter, or counsel to the king's declared enemies;
- an attempt on the life of the king, the queen, or the heir;
- slaying a member of the Kingsguard or the king's herald while he performs his duty;
- forging the royal seal or the king's coin, which is an offense against [royal decrees](crown_law.md#royal-decrees);
- breaking a peace sworn under the king's banner, as described in [violations by lords](war_and_siege_law.md#violations-by-lords).

A lord who openly defies a royal decree and refuses a summons may be charged with treason after a second refusal.

## Procedure

### Who May Try Treason

A lord, even a lord paramount, may not try a case of treason; see [limits on lordly power](lordly_justice.md#limits-on-lordly-power). The accused is brought to the king's court. The two-witness rule of [evidence and witnesses](courts_and_procedure.md#evidence-and-witnesses) applies in full, and the accused may speak last.

### Combat and Confession

An accused traitor who has not confessed may demand [trial by combat](trial_by_combat.md#when-it-may-be-demanded). The king names the accuser's champion if the Crown is the accuser. A traitor who confesses forfeits that right.

### Sentence

A noble traitor is beheaded. A commoner is hanged. The king alone may order a harsher death.

## Attainder and Forfeiture

Attainder is the legal consequence of a conviction for treason. The convicted person's lands, titles, and goods are forfeit to the Crown, and the blood of the traitor is considered corrupted: his heirs may not inherit. See [failure of heirs](succession_and_inheritance.md#failure-of-heirs).

### Extinction of a House

When the king declares a house extinct, its name is struck from the register kept by the Master of Laws, its banners are taken down, and its holdings may be granted to another house. A house is not extinct merely because its lord was attainted; the king must proclaim the extinction by decree.

### Restoration

An attainder may be lifted only by a sealed decree of restoration. A general pardon does not restore lands or titles; see [limits of the pardon](royal_mercy.md#limits-of-the-pardon).
