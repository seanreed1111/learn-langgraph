# Lordly Justice and the Rights of High and Low Justice

The great and lesser lords judge the people of their lands in the name of the king. The scope of that power depends on the grant under which the lord holds his land.

## High Justice and Low Justice

### The Right of Pit and Gallows

A lord who holds high justice has the right of pit and gallows: the power to condemn a man to death, to the loss of a hand, or to the Wall. Lords paramount, and any lord to whom the Crown has granted the right in writing, hold high justice. The name comes from the old custom of keeping a drowning pit and a gallows at the lord's seat.

### The Powers of Low Justice

A landed knight or a lesser lord without a grant holds only low justice. Low justice permits fines, the stocks, flogging up to twenty lashes, and imprisonment for up to a year and a day. A holder of low justice who needs a heavier penalty must send the accused, with the accusation in writing, to the nearest lord who holds high justice.

## The Lord as Judge

A lord may judge a case himself or appoint a steward or bailiff to sit for him. In a capital case the lord must sit in person, with a maester to record the proceedings and a septon to administer oaths. See [the judge and the panel](courts_and_procedure.md#the-judge-and-the-panel). In the North the lord who passes the sentence is expected to carry it out; see [the North](regional_customs.md#the-north).

## Lords Paramount

The lords paramount of the great realms hear appeals from the lords beneath them and may overturn a sentence of a lesser lord. Their judgments may in turn be appealed to the king under [appeals and royal review](courts_and_procedure.md#appeals-and-royal-review).

## Limits on Lordly Power

A lord may not:

- try a case of [treason](treason_and_attainder.md), which belongs to the Crown alone;
- try a member of the Kingsguard or the Faith, who answer to their own orders under [the Kingsguard](kingsguard_and_oaths.md) and [church courts](faith_and_septs.md#church-courts);
- deny a man his right to demand [trial by combat](trial_by_combat.md#when-it-may-be-demanded) in a capital case;
- punish a guest under his roof, which would break [guest right](guest_right.md).

A lord who abuses his justice may be summoned before his lord paramount, and a lord paramount may be summoned before the king. See also [the hierarchy of authority](crown_law.md#the-hierarchy-in-brief).
