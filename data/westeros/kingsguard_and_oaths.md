# The Kingsguard and the Law of Oaths

The Kingsguard is a brotherhood of seven knights sworn to protect the king. Their vows are among the most solemn in the law.

## The Sworn Brotherhood

The seven brothers of the Kingsguard are chosen by the king and led by the Lord Commander. A brother is a sworn knight who has given up his own lands and family claims to serve the king alone. A brother of the Kingsguard answers to the Lord Commander and to the king, and may not be tried by a lord; see [limits on lordly power](lordly_justice.md#limits-on-lordly-power).

## Vows

### The Oath

A brother swears to guard the king with his life, to obey the king's lawful commands, to keep the king's secrets, and to forswear lands, wives, and children. The oath is sworn before the Seven and is held to bind him for life.

### Effect on Property and Title

A brother who takes the vow gives up any claim to lordship and to inheritance. His lands pass to his heirs, and he may not marry. A bastard knighted for the Kingsguard also gives up all claim to legitimation; see [bastards in sworn orders](bastards_and_legitimation.md#bastards-in-sworn-orders).

## Release from Vows

Only the king may release a brother from his vows. A release must be written and sealed. The Faith holds that a vow sworn before the gods binds for life even if a king releases the man, so a released brother is often held in lesser honor. A brother who leaves without release is forsworn. Forsworn knights are not protected by the Kingsguard's privilege and may be tried by any lord, who may impose the penalties for [oathbreaking](#oathbreaking-in-general).

## Duties in Court

A brother of the Kingsguard stands guard at the king's side in court and in battle. A brother may serve as the king's champion in a [trial by combat](trial_by_combat.md#champions-and-substitutes) only with the king's leave, and may not fight for any other person. Slaying a brother of the Kingsguard in the course of his duty is [treason](treason_and_attainder.md#what-is-treason).

## Oathbreaking in General

An oath sworn before gods or a heart tree is binding in law. A person who breaks an oath of service loses his honor and may be fined, imprisoned, or banished at the discretion of the court. An oath of fealty broken by a lord is a breach of his duty to his liege, and if the liege is the king, it is treason. See also [guest right](guest_right.md) for the oath of hospitality and [perjury](crimes_and_punishments.md#perjury-and-false-witness) for false oaths in court.
