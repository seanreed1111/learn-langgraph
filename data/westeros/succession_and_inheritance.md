# Succession and Inheritance

This article governs the passing of lordships, lands, and goods on the death of a holder.

## Succession to Lordships

### Male-Preference Primogeniture

In most of the Seven Kingdoms a lordship passes to the eldest living son of the lord. If there is no son, it passes to the eldest living son's issue, then to the lord's younger sons in order of age, and then to the lord's daughters. A daughter's claim rests ahead of a brother of the lord. See [regional customs](regional_customs.md) for variations.

### Dornish Equal Primogeniture

In Dorne the eldest living child inherits regardless of sex. A daughter inherits before a younger brother. See [Dorne](regional_customs.md#dorne).

### The Iron Islands

On the Iron Islands the eldest son inherits, but a lord who cannot hold his seat may be displaced by the captains' moot. See [the Iron Islands](regional_customs.md#the-iron-islands).

## Failure of Heirs

If a lord leaves no heir of his body, the lordship passes to his nearest kin by blood. If there is no kin, the lordship returns to the lord paramount, who may grant it anew. A lordship forfeit through [attainder](treason_and_attainder.md#attainder-and-forfeiture) is held by the Crown and may be granted only by the king.

## Regency and Wardship

An heir under sixteen years of age may not hold a lordship in his own name. A regent governs on his behalf. The regent is the person named in the late lord's will or, failing that, the heir's nearest adult male kin. An orphaned heir is the ward of the lord paramount unless the will names another guardian. See also [fostering](marriage_and_family_law.md#wardship-and-fostering).

## Disputed Succession

When two persons claim the same lordship, the lord paramount decides the dispute. His judgment may be appealed to the king under [appeals and royal review](courts_and_procedure.md#appeals-and-royal-review). A claimant who takes up arms before the judgment is given is charged with an offense against the king's peace.

## Property and Dower

### Landed Property

Land passes with the lordship and cannot be divided without the lord paramount's consent. A lord may grant lesser lands to younger sons, but the main seat passes whole.

### Movable Goods and Dower

Movable goods are divided by the will. A widow keeps her dower, which is one third of her husband's movable goods and a life interest in the lands set aside for her at marriage. See [dowry and dower](marriage_and_family_law.md#dowry-and-dower).

## Bastards and Inheritance

A bastard cannot inherit a lordship unless he is legitimized. See [legitimation](bastards_and_legitimation.md#legitimation).
